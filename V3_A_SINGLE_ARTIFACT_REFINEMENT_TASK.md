## Pixel-level refinement brief

Address exactly these issues, in priority order:

### 1. Typography / identity rail

The left vertical rail is too dominant and somewhat generic/old-fashioned.
Refine it so it feels contemporary, brand-specific and integrated with the image system.

- reduce the feeling of a pre-made vertical restaurant template;
- improve character grouping, scale rhythm and optical spacing;
- keep the exact Chinese headline content unless an existing source requires otherwise;
- do not turn it into oversized generic brush calligraphy;
- keep supporting text subordinate and legible.

### 2. Product mass balance

The lower-right plated dish currently occupies too much visual mass.

- reduce its dominance without making the food feel small or weak;
- create more breathing room and stronger dialogue between process scene, identity rail and dish;
- retain strong food realism and appetizing texture;
- do not replace the real product identity with a different dish.

### 3. Secondary narrative modules

The circular image windows/gold strokes currently read partially as collage/moodboard devices.

- simplify and unify them into one consistent semantic graphic system;
- reduce decorative flourish that does not carry meaning;
- keep only the strongest ingredient/process/dining cues;
- avoid scrapbook, sticker or moodboard styling.

### 4. Dark-field / color cleanup

The dark field is too brown/sooty in areas.

- preserve warm fire light, but make blacks cleaner and more separated;
- improve shadow hierarchy and local subject separation;
- reduce muddy brown contamination;
- keep red as a controlled accent, not a wash over the whole piece.

### 5. Microcopy / icons

Small English labels and icons currently feel generic.

- make them quieter and more systematic;
- reduce stock-icon / generic food-poster feel;
- if a supporting label cannot be rendered reliably, simplify rather than inventing clutter;
- do not add more informational elements merely to fill space.
