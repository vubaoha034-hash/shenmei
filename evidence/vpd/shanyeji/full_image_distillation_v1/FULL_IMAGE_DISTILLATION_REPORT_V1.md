## Photography grammar

The image is an immersive, full-bleed environmental photograph with no visible horizon. A large soft lower-left foreground mass, sharper central/right foliage and dark receding background create at least three depth stages. The crop is asymmetric and edge-pressured; vegetation enters and exits every edge while darker central/top/bottom pockets function as information-support fields.

Focus is selective: the lower-left near field is strongly soft, the middle foliage is the clearest, and the background recedes with moderate softness. Lighting is broad and low-specularity with dispersed foliage highlights rather than a hard cast-shadow system. Photographic highlights remain below the light typography in luminance authority.

The tonal system is dominated by deep green-black and middle green with scarce photographic highlights. Regional contrast—not a flat dark overlay—protects information. Natural edge variation, bounded microcontrast and selective sharpness preserve photographic realism.

## Photo-design integration grammar

The photograph and design are coupled through local frequency and luminance. Small information uses quiet dark pockets; the display object can cross richer foliage because it carries more area and contrast. A broad title mass counterbalances an edge-to-edge active photograph while leaving the environment identity-bearing.

Depth supports information: soft near mass and dark background frame a readable middle plane. Photographic light is constrained so typography owns the highest highlights. Limited accent color may connect semantic roles but is optional. Graphics do not repaint or globally texture the photograph, preserving real foliage edges and depth.

The key integration rule is regional density coordination: high photographic detail and high graphic detail are not maximized everywhere simultaneously.
